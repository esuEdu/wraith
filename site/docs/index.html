wraith documentation

Everything wraith does, how to use it, and what it changes on your Mac.

Overview

wraith is a proxy you run from the terminal. While it runs, it is your Mac's HTTP and HTTPS proxy. It decrypts and shows traffic from one capture source: the iOS Simulator, an iPhone or iPad on your Wi-Fi, one Mac app, one process, or everything. Traffic from anything else passes through untouched: never decrypted, stored or shown. For the app you capture, it can also answer requests with mocks and slow the network.

Requirements: macOS 13 or later on Apple Silicon or Intel. Xcode with at least one iOS simulator to capture simulator traffic.

wraith keeps its files in ~/.wraith. Set WRAITH_HOME to use another folder, for example a throwaway one for testing.

Install

Homebrew

wraith installs from its own tap. Homebrew builds it from the release's source on your Mac, so there is no Gatekeeper prompt. brew upgrade wraith gets new releases.

brew install esuEdu/tap/wraith
wraith version

From source

Needs Go (the repository pins its toolchain; go fetches the right one).

git clone https://github.com/esuEdu/wraith.git
cd wraith
make build
./wraith version

Tab completion

Tab completes commands, flags and their values: wraith st becomes wraith start, and wraith start --source lists the booted simulators, the apps running in them and on the Mac, and remembered devices. devices forget, ca install --sim and --iface complete from what is on your Mac too. Homebrew sets it up for zsh, bash and fish; open a new terminal after installing. Built from source, add it yourself, for zsh:

wraith completion zsh > "$(brew --prefix)/share/zsh/site-functions/_wraith"

wraith completion --help shows bash and fish. If Tab does nothing in zsh, your ~/.zshrc needs autoload -Uz compinit && compinit.

To remove wraith later, see Uninstall: take its CA out of everything that trusts it first.

Quick start

  1. Boot a simulator: xcrun simctl boot "iPhone 17" && open -a Simulator.
  2. Run wraith start. The first run creates ~/.wraith/config.yaml and the CA, puts the CA into every booted simulator, sets the system proxy, and opens the live view.
  3. Use your app in the simulator. Its requests appear as they complete, decrypted.
  4. Press q (or Ctrl-C). wraith stops and restores your proxy settings.

Check your setup at any time with wraith doctor.

Only want to listen? wraith start --no-system-proxy leaves the system proxy alone; point a client at 127.0.0.1:9090 yourself, for example curl -x http://127.0.0.1:9090 https://example.com.

Capture sources

The source decides which connections wraith decrypts and shows. Choose it with --source or source: in the config. Everything outside it is passed through untouched and never shown.

SourceCaptures
simulatorEvery booted iOS simulator. The default.
simulator:iPhone 17
simulator:<UDID>		One simulator, by device name or UDID.
app:Safari
app:com.example.shop		One app, by name or bundle id; also an app inside a simulator (the picker lists them under their simulator; the name matches the app in any simulator). A command-line tool matches by its executable name, e.g. app:curl.
pid:4242One process and every process it started.
device
device:192.168.0.23		Every iPhone or iPad you allowed, or one by its IP address. Needs --device; see iPhone and iPad.
allEverything that uses the system proxy, and every device you allowed.

How wraith knows who connected

For each new connection, wraith asks the kernel which process owns the client's socket, then walks its parent processes. A process running under launchd_sim belongs to a simulator, whose UDID comes from launchd_sim's arguments. An app is the outermost .app bundle on the process's own path, so Chrome's network helper (a bundle inside Chrome.app) counts as Chrome. A connection whose process cannot be identified is outside every source.

Pages loaded in a simulator's Safari or in a web view come from com.apple.WebKit.Networking, not from the app itself, and are labelled that way.

Change the source of a running wraith with s in the live view. It applies to new connections.

iPhone and iPad

A physical device on the same Wi-Fi as your Mac can use wraith as its proxy. Its traffic then leaves through your Mac, so it goes through your Mac's VPN if one is up.

wraith start --device

wraith also listens on your Mac's Wi-Fi address, for example 192.168.0.12:9090, and the live view shows a QR code with the steps (d shows it again later). The source is device unless you give --source.

  1. Scan the QR code with the iPhone's Camera and open the link. The device is now allowed, and the page shows the rest.
  2. Tap Download the certificate, install it from Settings › Profile Downloaded, then turn on full trust for wraith CA in Settings › General › About › Certificate Trust Settings. Once per device.
  3. In Settings › Wi-Fi, tap ⓘ next to the network, then Configure Proxy › Manual, and enter the server and port the page shows.

Turn the proxy off on the device when wraith is not running, or it loses its connection.

A phone has no process wraith can ask, so each request from it is labelled with the app its User-Agent names: Shop/2.3 CFNetwork/… Darwin/… shows as device 192.168.0.23 · Shop, a browser User-Agent as Safari or web view. Filter on it with /, e.g. /Shop. It is a hint, not proof: an app may send any User-Agent, and a tunnel that is not decrypted shows only what its CONNECT request said.

Who can connect

The QR code carries a key that is new each time wraith starts. A device on your Mac's network that opens the link with it is allowed, and remembered: on later runs it gets in without the code. wraith remembers it by its IP address on that network, in ~/.wraith/devices.json (macOS does not show wraith the network's hardware addresses), and forgets it after 30 days unseen. At a new address, it needs the QR code again. wraith devices list shows the remembered devices and wraith devices forget <ip|all> removes them. Another device that later gets a remembered address from the router could use wraith too; forget devices you no longer use.

wraith listens on that one Wi-Fi address only, never on every address, and only while --device is on. It hands the CA certificate only to allowed devices; its private key never leaves the Mac.

When it does not work

  • The device and the Mac must be on the same network. Guest and many office networks keep devices apart; a phone hotspot or home Wi-Fi works.
  • If the macOS firewall blocks wraith, the live view says so: set wraith to “Allow incoming connections” in System Settings › Network › Firewall › Options.
  • A VPN on the device can send its traffic away from your Wi-Fi; turn it off while you use wraith.
  • Apps that pin their certificates fail when decrypted. Add their hosts to no_decrypt (n in the live view).

HTTPS and the CA

wraith decrypts HTTPS with its own certificate authority, created on your Mac on first use in ~/.wraith/ca/. The folder is mode 0700 and the key 0600; wraith refuses to start if either is wider. The key never leaves the folder. The CA is valid for two years; wraith doctor warns 30 days before it expires.

Simulators

wraith start installs the CA into every booted simulator, and into each simulator booted while it runs, within a few seconds. wraith ca install does it on demand; wraith ca remove takes it out of every simulator again, booted or not, without touching other certificates. A booted simulator may keep trusting a removed CA until it restarts.

The Mac

By default the Mac does not trust the wraith CA, so Mac apps in the source are passed through and shown, but not decrypted. To decrypt them:

wraith ca trust --mac      # explains what this allows, asks, then macOS asks for your password
wraith ca untrust --mac    # undo

Think before trusting. While the Mac trusts the CA, wraith can decrypt HTTPS from every app that uses the system trust store, and anyone who obtains ~/.wraith/ca/ca.key could impersonate any website to your Mac.

When a connection is not decrypted

A tunnel that wraith passes through shows as tunnel in the log, with the reason:

  • on no_decrypt: the host matches the no_decrypt list (by default *.apple.com and *.icloud.com). Add a host from the live view with n.
  • certificate rejected: the client refused wraith's certificate, because the app pins its certificates or the device does not trust the wraith CA yet. That attempt fails, and wraith passes the host through for that client for 30 seconds, then tries again; after a third refusal it stops trying for the session. So a device you have just set up is decrypted again within half a minute, and an app that pins fails only a few times. Add a pinned host to no_decrypt (n) to pass it through from the start.
  • the client sent nothing: the client opened a tunnel and stayed silent for 10 seconds, usually a browser's preconnect.
  • not TLS: another protocol over CONNECT, such as SSH.
  • this client is not decrypted: a Mac app while the Mac does not trust the CA.

wraith always verifies the real server's certificate (TLS 1.2 or later). For a local development server with a self-signed certificate, name it: wraith start --insecure-upstream dev.local.test.

Rotate the CA with wraith ca rotate: it removes the old one from simulators and installs the new one into booted ones. Export the certificate (never the key) for a device you set up by hand with wraith ca export ca.pem.

Live view

wraith start opens the live view when your terminal is interactive. Each row shows the time, the source, the status (tun for a tunnel passed through, with why after its address: no_decrypt, certificate rejected, idle…), the method, via (mock when a mock answered, or the slow-network profile it went through) and the URL. The list follows new traffic unless you scroll up. It draws in the Heliotrope Haze theme, a dark violet palette; set theme: terminal in the configuration to use your terminal's own 16 colors instead, so it follows your terminal theme, light or dark.

KeyDoes
↑ ↓ · j kMove. g / G jump to the oldest / newest.
EnterDetails, with a Response tab and a Request tab, each showing headers and body, a Timing tab, and for a WebSocket a Messages tab. Tab (or ← →, 1–4) switches tabs. Timing draws where the time went as a waterfall: blocked (in wraith, or a slow-network profile's latency), DNS, connect, TLS, send, wait for the first byte, and receive; a reused connection skips DNS, connect and TLS. Messages lists what the app (↑) and the server (↓) sent, as they pass: text as it is, binary by size, pings and closes with their code, compressed messages (permessage-deflate) decompressed; the last 1,000 are kept, and body: searches them. gzip and deflate bodies are decoded and JSON is pretty-printed and colored, for display only; traffic is forwarded as sent. In the details, b copies the body of the tab on screen (decoded), h its headers, u the URL and c the request as curl; copied headers and URLs are redacted like exports. Esc goes back.
/Filter. Every term must match: a plain word in the source, method, URL, status or via (mock, 3g); body:text inside the request or response body, decoded as the details show it; header:text in any header name or value; op:name a GraphQL operation (op:GetCart, or op:mutation). Quotes keep a phrase together: body:"user id". For example api.shop body:A04FBEEB. Tab completes the word you are typing from what has been captured (hosts, sources and apps, methods, statuses, header names after header:, and GraphQL operations after op:); with several matches it lists them, and each Tab steps to the next. Esc clears it.
sPick the capture source for new connections: all, then a group each for Simulators (every one, or one with its iOS version and whether it has the CA, with the apps running in it listed under it), Mac apps and, with --device, iPhone & iPad. Groups start folded, showing how many they hold; the one with the current source starts open. Enter, → or a click opens a group, ← folds it, and a click or Enter on a source captures it. Typing searches every group at once; text that matches nothing is used as typed, e.g. pid:1234.
dWith --device: show the QR code and steps to set up an iPhone or iPad again. Esc goes back to the flows.
nAdd the selected host to no_decrypt.
cCopy the request as a curl command, secrets redacted.
rSend the selected request again, also from the details: same method, URL, headers and body, with the upstream certificate verified. The answer is a new row, its source marked replay. A request that may change data (anything but GET, HEAD and OPTIONS) asks for a second r. A tunnel, or a body cut at capture.max_body_bytes, cannot be sent again.
mMock the selected request: from now on wraith answers it with this response instead of asking the server. Its body goes to a file in ~/.wraith/mocks to edit; see Mocks. m on a mocked request stops mocking it. Also in the details.
=Compare two requests: = marks the selected one (◆), = on another shows what changed from the first to the second, part by part: the request line, headers and body, then the status, headers and body of the response. JSON is compared with its keys sorted, so only real differences show. s swaps the two, Esc goes back, and = on the marked one unmarks it. Also in the details.
tSlow the network: each press steps through the profiles lte, 3g, edge, very-bad and back to off. The header shows the one in use.
eExport every flow as HAR to wraith-<time>.har in the current folder, redacted.
xClear the list.
q · Ctrl-CQuit and restore the system proxy.

Log mode and JSON

With --log, or when the output is not a terminal, wraith prints one line per flow instead:

12:04:31 [sim iPhone 17 · Shop] GET https://api.shop.example/v2/cart?page=2 200 1.2 kB 34ms
12:04:32 [sim iPhone 17 · Shop] GET https://api.shop.example/v2/recs 503 64 B 2ms mock
12:04:32 [curl] CONNECT example.com:443 tunnel (passed through: this client is not decrypted …)

Lines carry the time, source, method, URL, status, size, duration and via (mock, or a slow-network profile such as 3g), never a header value or a body. A WebSocket's messages follow as lines of their own with their direction, kind and size, never their content. Query parameters named like secrets are redacted and query strings are cut after 64 characters. --log --json prints the same fields as one JSON object per line; via is the via field (wraith 1.x called it route).

GraphQL

A GraphQL app sends every request to one endpoint, so the URL says little. wraith reads the operation from the request instead (its operationName and query, a batch of them, a persisted query by its name, or ?query= on a GET) and shows it on the row: https://api.shop.example/graphql query GetCart. GraphQL answers 200 even when an operation fails, so a response with errors is marked ✗ 2 errors.

  • The Request tab shows the query and its variables on their own, formatted, above the raw body.
  • op:GetCart in the filter keeps one operation; Tab completes operation names.
  • m on a GraphQL request mocks that operation only, with operation: GetCart in its rule; other operations on the same endpoint still reach the server.
  • Log lines, JSON log lines and HAR exports name the operation too.

Mocks

A mock answers a request of the capture source itself, without asking the server: to see the app's error screen, an empty state, or a feature the backend does not have yet. Mocked rows show mock in the via column. Only requests wraith decrypts (or plain http://) can be mocked.

The quickest way is m on a request in the live view: wraith saves its response body to a file in ~/.wraith/mocks, named after the URL, and adds a rule for it to config.yaml. Edit the file to change the body; the change applies to the next request, without restarting. Press m on it again to stop.

Rules live in config.yaml, and edits to them apply within a second. The first rule that matches answers:

mocks:
  - match: "GET https://api.shop.example/v2/cart"   # [METHOD ]URL
    status: 503
    headers: {Content-Type: application/json}
    body: '{"error": "try again later"}'
    delay: 2s                                       # wait before answering
  - match: "https://api.shop.example/v2/items/*"     # any method; * is anything
    file: items.json                                 # in ~/.wraith/mocks
  - match: "POST https://api.shop.example/graphql"
    operation: GetCart                               # this GraphQL operation only
    file: cart.json
  - match: "GET https://api.shop.example/v2/promo"
    file: promo.json
    off: true                                        # kept, but the server answers
  • match is a URL, with an optional method in front. * matches anything. A URL without ? matches any query string; with one, the query must match too.
  • status defaults to 200. body and file are exclusive; file is a file name inside ~/.wraith/mocks, never a path. A file that cannot be read answers 502, and the row says why.
  • delay is at most one minute. Mocks are not slowed by the slow-network profile; use delay instead.

Slow network

A profile slows the capture source's traffic, to see loading states, spinners and timeouts: every request and tunnel waits the profile's latency before it goes out, and all of the source's traffic shares its bandwidth, as on a slow network. Anything outside the capture source is not slowed. Xcode's Network Link Conditioner slows the whole Mac; this only slows what you are capturing.

ProfileLatencyDownUp
lte50 ms12 Mbit/s5 Mbit/s
3g100 ms780 kbit/s330 kbit/s
edge400 ms240 kbit/s200 kbit/s
very-bad500 ms100 kbit/s100 kbit/s

Start with one (wraith start --throttle 3g, or throttle: 3g in config.yaml), or press t in the live view to step through them. Slowed rows say throttled 3g in their details, and the Timing tab shows the latency as blocked time.

Export

Captured flows live only in the running wraith's memory (the last 5,000 by default, bodies kept up to 1 MB each). Save them while it runs:

wraith export --har flows.har
  • The file is HAR 1.2: open it in a browser's network panel or any HAR viewer. Each entry notes its source, a mock that answered it or a slow-network profile it went through, has the timing phases the Timing tab shows, and a WebSocket's messages in the form Chrome's DevTools reads.
  • Authorization, Cookie, Set-Cookie and *token* headers, and query parameters with those names, are replaced by REDACTED. --no-redact keeps them.
  • The file is written with mode 0600 and never over an existing one without --force.
  • export talks to the running wraith over a Unix socket in ~/.wraith that only you can open. When wraith is not running there is nothing to export.

Configuration

~/.wraith/config.yaml is created with these defaults on first run. Command-line flags override it. Unknown keys and invalid values are refused when wraith starts.

port: 9090                  # proxy port on 127.0.0.1 and ::1
source: simulator           # all | simulator | simulator:<udid|name> | app:<name|bundle-id> | pid:<pid>
interface: auto             # the Wi-Fi or Ethernet interface --device listens on: auto, or en0, en1, ...

capture:
  max_flows: 5000           # flows kept in memory
  max_body_bytes: 1048576   # bodies above this are truncated


no_decrypt:                 # always passed through, never decrypted
  - "*.apple.com"
  - "*.icloud.com"

export:
  redact_headers: [Authorization, Cookie, Set-Cookie, "*token*"]

theme: heliotrope-haze      # live view colors: heliotrope-haze, or terminal for its own palette

throttle: off               # slow network for the capture source: off, lte, 3g, edge, very-bad

mocks: []                   # answers instead of the server; see Mocks

Security and privacy

  • wraith listens on 127.0.0.1 and ::1 only and closes any other peer before reading from it. With --device it also listens on the Mac's Wi-Fi address, and lets in only devices on that network that opened the setup link from its QR code.
  • It runs as you and refuses to run as root; macOS asks for permissions itself when it needs to.
  • Captured traffic stays in memory and is gone when wraith stops. Only exports you ask for write files, mode 0600, redacted by default.
  • Log lines never contain header values or bodies.
  • Every upstream certificate is verified, except for hosts you name with --insecure-upstream.
  • wraith makes no network calls of its own: no telemetry, no update checks.
  • Your proxy settings are saved before any change and restored on exit, Ctrl-C, SIGTERM and after a crash.

Report a vulnerability privately through GitHub, never in a public issue. See the security policy.

Troubleshooting

Start with wraith doctor; it names what is wrong and how to fix it.

Websites stopped loading after wraith exited

The system proxy still points at wraith (for example after a forced quit). Run wraith stop: it restores your settings even when wraith is not running. On an iPhone or iPad, set its Wi-Fi proxy back to Off.

An app's requests do not appear

  • Is it in the source? The default is simulator; Mac apps need app:<name> or all.
  • Does it use the system proxy? Apps that ignore proxy settings bypass wraith completely.
  • A web page in the simulator shows as com.apple.WebKit.Networking, not as the app.
  • On macOS 27.2 or later, use wraith 1.5.2 or later. Earlier versions cannot tell which app opened a connection there, so they pass everything through. wraith version shows yours; brew upgrade wraith updates it.

A host shows as tunnel instead of being decrypted

The row says why after its address: no_decrypt, certificate rejected (the app pins, or a device does not trust the CA yet; tried again after 30 seconds), idle, not TLS, or a Mac app the Mac does not trust the CA for. See When a connection is not decrypted.

The simulator does not trust the CA

Run wraith ca install with the simulator booted, then restart the app. wraith doctor lists each booted simulator and whether it trusts the CA.

wraith warns about a bypass list

Your config.yaml still has the bypass: list of wraith 1.x. It is ignored; delete the line to stop the warning.

Port 9090 is in use

Use another one: wraith start --port 9191, or set port: in the config.

Limits

  • macOS only. iPhones and iPads are captured only on the same Wi-Fi as the Mac, with --device; Android devices are not supported.
  • Apps that ignore the system proxy are not captured.
  • wraith can answer requests with mocks, but does not edit traffic in flight: no breakpoints, and no rewriting of a real response.
  • WebSockets are shown over HTTP/1.1, the way iOS opens them; WebSockets over HTTP/2 are not.
  • HTTP/3 (QUIC) is not proxied; clients fall back to HTTP/2 or HTTP/1.1 through the proxy.
  • wraith does not route traffic around a VPN. wraith 1.x could, for hosts on a bypass: list; that was removed in v2.0.0, because a VPN that enforces its routes blocks it. wraith bypass now only says so.

Uninstall

Remove wraith in this order: everything that trusts its CA first, then the program, then its folder. The folder holds the CA key, so delete it last; after that, the steps that need wraith no longer work.

  1. Stop it and restore your proxy settings. Safe to run even if wraith is not running: it also puts back settings a crash left behind. 
    wraith stop
  2. Take the CA out of every simulator, booted or not. Other certificates stay. 
    wraith ca remove
  3. If you made the Mac trust it (wraith ca trust --mac), take it out of the Mac keychain. macOS asks for your password. 
    wraith ca untrust --mac
  4. On each iPhone or iPad you set up with --device:
    • Settings › Wi-Fi › ⓘ next to the network › Configure Proxy › Off.
    • Settings › General › VPN & Device Management › wraith CA › Remove Profile.
  5. Remove the program. With Homebrew, also remove the tap if you have nothing else from it: 
    brew uninstall wraith
brew untap esuEdu/tap
    Built from source: delete the wraith binary you built.
  6. Delete its folder: the CA and its key, config.yaml, remembered devices and the saved proxy settings. This cannot be undone. 
    rm -rf ~/.wraith
    If you set WRAITH_HOME, delete that folder instead.

Check it is gone. System Settings › Network › your network › Details › Proxies shows no proxy left on; which wraith prints nothing.
